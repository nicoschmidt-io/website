In more than 1,700 public schools in the Brazilian state of Paraná, facial recognition takes the register. The teacher photographs the class and the software matches the faces. It comes from Innovatrics, a company based in Bratislava. In Europe, regulators and courts have stopped similar school trials; Sweden even fined a school board over one in 2019.

Students the system fails to recognise are marked absent. Because attendance is linked to the Bolsa Família welfare programme, mistakes could cost families money. One study found an accuracy rate of 91.1 percent, below the 95 percent the contract requires. Prosecutors have taken the state to court; the state says the system is lawful. The EU has no ban on exporting this kind of technology.
